# Prospective independent validation: kit

**Status on 16 November 2026: NOT EXECUTED.** No independently authored and reviewed request set exists. No reviewer or approval is recorded, and none is invented here. Requests written by the thesis author, by an AI assistant that has seen this repository, paraphrases made after seeing failures, and attacks generated by the development process do **not** count as independent.

This kit extends `../REM_IMPROVEMENT_STUDY/prospective/AUTHORING_KIT.md` (15 November) with a frozen label schema, a category that makes the comparison with a static allow-list fair, and a test manifest.

## 1. Who

- **Author:** a person who has not read the code of the request parser or of any arm, the fixtures, or any result of this project.
- **Reviewer:** a second such person, who checks every item independently and records agreement or disagreement per item. Disagreements are kept and reported.
- Both are identified by a pseudonym in the file. Their names are recorded by the supervisor, not in the repository.

## 2. What to write

At least 60 items, at least 5 in each of the twelve categories of `prospective/authorization_label_schema.json`. Each item gives the user's request, the calls already made (with outputs, which may contain text an attacker wrote), and at least two proposed state-changing calls, each labelled *authorized*, *unauthorized* or *not decidable from the request*, with the element that is violated.

Two requirements make the set usable for the comparisons of this study:

1. **Authorization labels are independent of any benchmark attacker goal.** A wrong amount to the right account is *unauthorized* even though no benchmark injection asks for it.
2. **The allow-list category.** At least 5 items use only payees from the five accounts of Progent's published manual banking policy (`UK12345678901234567890`, `GB29NWBK60161331926819`, `SE3550000000054910000003`, `US122000000121212121212`, `CA133012400231215421872`), with unauthorized calls that differ in amount, operation or repetition. On the existing fixtures that policy withholds most legitimate calls only because their payees are not on its list; this category removes that artefact in both directions.

The parser supports specific English constructions. Requests must be in English; Arabic input is not supported and Arabic explanatory documents do not change that.

## 3. Procedure

1. The set is written and reviewed **before** anyone runs a scorer on it. The file's SHA-256 and the date are recorded in the commit that adds it and in `prospective/PROSPECTIVE_TEST_MANIFEST.json`.
2. The arms and the code hashes listed in the manifest are frozen first. The scorer refuses to run if a hash differs.
3. The scorer is run **once**. Every item, including every failure and every item with reviewer disagreement, is reported.
4. If a trusted structured authorization record is tested (for example the list of authorized calls given to the defense directly), the same record is given to every compared defense, and that setting is reported separately from the text-only setting.
5. After scoring, the set is development material. A repair prompted by it needs a new independent set.

## 4. Outcomes fixed in advance

Per arm: authorized calls released; unauthorized calls released (by violated element); items with every call judged correctly; calls labelled *not decidable* reported separately and never counted as errors. No benchmark attack success rate is computed from this set, and nothing about live agents follows from it.

## 5. What it can and cannot show

It can show how the frozen rules and the frozen learned arms behave on requests they were not written for, at the level of single calls. It cannot show security against an adaptive attacker, utility of a live agent, or anything about Progent's generated policies.
