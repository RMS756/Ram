# 03: Literature and research-gap audit

## Method and its limits

**Records.** Bibliographic records were checked in this session through the scholarly index (OpenAlex via the literature connector) and Crossref.

**Mechanism claims** are taken from the highest evidence level available:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| F | Full text read (in an earlier session; noted where so) |
| R | Official repository code read; for ROPE also the authors' run logs |
| A | Abstract |
| S | Search excerpt of the full text |

**Constraint.** Full texts on `arxiv.org` and `mdpi.com` could not be opened from this session (DNS refusal; documented in `REM_X_DESIGN_AMENDMENT_01/01`). The instruction to verify against the original paper could therefore be met only for:
- Progent (F, earlier session);
- ROPE, CaMeL and MELON (R).

**Everything else is at level A or S and is marked so.** No mechanism is described as absent from a paper read only at A/S. Such cells read "not determined".

## 2. Is the core idea already addressed?

**Idea examined.** "A layered runtime security framework that protects financial AI agents against indirect prompt injection by combining:
- context-level inspection,
- behavioural monitoring,
- tool-action assessment,
- risk-informed decisions, and
- mitigation at the execution boundary."

| Element | Already established? | Representative prior work (level) |
|---|---|---|
| Context-level inspection of untrusted content | **Yes** | AgentDojo's PI detector; PromptArmor; MELON's detection; Spotlight-Guard (A, R) |
| Behavioural / action-sequence monitoring | **Yes** | Agent-Sentry learned sequence bound; AGATE effect ledger; DRIFT plan-deviation validator (A) |
| Tool-action assessment and authorization at the boundary | **Yes, extensively** | Progent (F), ROPE (R), CaMeL (R), AgentCap, ClawGuard, CapLite, IPIGuard (A) |
| Risk-informed (calibrated, graded) intervention | **Yes** | NEXUS: calibrated LR + allow/block/confirm/revise (peer reviewed, A); SIEVE (escalation); CASEL (cost-weighted modes, per thesis) |
| Mitigation before execution | **Yes** | All of the above |
| **Layering several of these** | **Yes** | Agent-Sentry (3 layers), Spotlight-Guard (3 layers, peer reviewed, with per-layer ablation and an adaptive attack), NEXUS (rules + inspection + risk), AGATE, AgentSandbox (DiD as principle) |
| **Financial specialization** | **Partly** | AIS is finance-specific (peer reviewed). Every AgentDojo evaluation includes the banking suite. Banking exfiltration studied (row 23) |

**Conclusion on the idea.** The idea as stated is **substantially addressed** by prior work, both element by element and as a layered combination. A gap cannot be claimed for it. This holds even allowing that several works were read only at abstract level: the abstracts alone already state the combinations.

## 3. What remains insufficiently addressed (evidence gaps, not mechanism gaps)

These are candidate gaps. Each is phrased as a question that evidence can answer. **Each is bounded by the A/S reading level of most works.**

| # | Candidate gap | Why it may be open | What REM's evidence can say |
|---|---|---|---|
| G-a | **Does a learned, calibrated, consequence-aware decision add value over deterministic provenance or authorization rules for a banking agent, under held-out tasks and attacks?** | NEXUS reports gains over rule-only on its own (mostly synthetic) benchmarks. Close works report fitting per benchmark (thesis Ch. 1 §1.4). The question of controlled comparison on AgentDojo banking with held-out objectives is not answered in the abstracts read | **REM's strongest evidence.** Without authorization, the learned decision supplies all of REM v1.0's protection, but at a large benign cost. Behind deterministic authorization it adds nothing, in every study that tested it. Simple rules and Progent's manual policy match or beat it in replay (02) |
| G-b | **How much of a layered defense's protection survives when one layer is evaded rather than removed?** | Per-layer ablation exists (Spotlight-Guard, Progent, AIS, CaMeL variants). Test-time evasion of one layer with the others fixed was not found at the levels read | REM-DiD DiD-2/DiD-3 measured this, **exploratorily and with modelled evasion**: removal and evasion gave opposite verdicts on one component family; the controls are largely redundant (02 §5) |
| G-c | **How should replay-based (non-adaptive) evaluation of action-boundary defenses be read?** | AutoDojo and Bhagwatkar et al. warn about static benchmarks. A specific measurement pitfall of NO-ADAPT replay (utility insensitive to read-only completions) was not found reported | This audit found that REM v1.0's benign replay utility on P1 **equals the withhold-everything floor episode by episode** (02 §1.3). This is a methodological finding about replay evaluation |

**Gap that cannot be claimed:**
- "No prior work combines context, behaviour, action, risk and mitigation in layers for financial agents" is **not supported**.
- The thesis's own Chapter 1 §1.4 already says the gap "is not the absence of a mechanism". **That position is correct and should be kept.**
